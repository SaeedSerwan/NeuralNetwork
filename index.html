Why neural networks? Learning instead of rules.

Some problems are easy for people and nearly impossible to program by hand. This lecture shows how a neural network solves them by learning from examples, built up one idea at a time over ten short chapters. Hover any underlined term or formula for an explanation.

To build a smart machine, copy the brain. A real neuron collects signals from other neurons through connections (synapses) of different strengths, adds them up, and fires only above a threshold. The artificial neuron copies exactly these four ideas.

03The whole idea first: how a network learns

Before any detail, the big picture. A network learns like a child learning to read: guess, get corrected, adjust. At the start all weights are random, so the guesses are wrong. Each round makes the weights a little better. Every page of this site is one piece of this loop.

step 1Guessrun the input through the network (forward pass)
step 2Measurecompare with the right answer: the cost
step 3Find the directionwhich way should each weight move? (the gradient)
step 4Nudgemove every weight a little that way (gradient descent)

Keep this loop in mind: every chapter is one piece of it. Chapters 2 and 3 open up step 1 (what is inside the network). Chapters 4 and 5 explain steps 2–4. In chapter 6 you will watch the whole loop run.
