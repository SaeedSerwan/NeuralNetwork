01How to read the 3D view

The bottom layer is your drawing: 784 squares, one per pixel. Above it are the 300 neurons of the first hidden layer, the 100 of the second, and at the top the 10 output neurons, one per digit. A brighter square is a more active neuron. The lines show the strongest contributions flowing upwards: green adds to the neuron above, red subtracts. Click any neuron to see its three steps, exactly as in chapter 2: weighted input (each input × its weight), calculation (add them up, add the bias) and output (ReLU, or softmax for the 10 outputs).

02Backward pass and learning

Choose the correct digit and press Backward pass. The error starts at the top: δ = output − wanted, orange where the output is too high, blue where it is too low. It then flows down the same connections, weighted by the same weights, layer by layer: this is backpropagation (chapter 5). Neurons that were inactive (ReLU = 0) pass no blame. Learn from this drawing then moves every weight by −η × input × δ and shows how the probability of the correct digit changes.

Train from random weights wipes all 266,610 knobs and trains the network in your browser on 5,000 digits with mini-batches of 16, while you watch the layers reorganise and the test accuracy rise: the loop of chapter 6 on a network 20 times larger.
