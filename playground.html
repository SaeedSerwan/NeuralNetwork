01The playground

You know how a network computes and how it learns. Now experiment: change the data, the layers, the activation and the learning rate, and see what each one does. Each square is a neuron from chapter 2; the lines are its weights; Play runs the training loop from chapter 6.

Each square in the network is one neuron. Its little picture shows its activation for every point of the plane: blue where it is high, orange where it is low (or negative). The lines are weights: blue positive, orange negative, thicker = larger. The big picture on the right is the output neuron: the network’s decision boundary.

    03What the playground shows

    one neuron

    A straight line

    With no hidden layer, the output is σ(w₁x₁ + w₂x₂ + b): blue on one side of a straight line, orange on the other.

    hidden layers

    Lines combined into curves

    Each hidden neuron draws its own line; the next layer mixes them into corners, bands and closed shapes.

    activation

    The bend

    With “none”, any number of layers still draws only a straight line. The activation is what allows curves.

    train vs test

    Overfitting

    With few training points and a big network, the training cost keeps falling while the test cost rises: it memorises the noise.
