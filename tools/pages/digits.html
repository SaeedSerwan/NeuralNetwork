02What did our network actually learn?

Remember the hope from Part 1: edges in the first hidden layer, loops and lines in the second. Each picture below shows the 784 weights of one first-layer neuron, arranged as a 28 × 28 image (green +, red −). The 16 neurons of hidden layer 1

A few neurons look for strokes in one area; most look like noise. The network found a solution that works (about 95% correct), not the tidy one we imagined. Real edge detectors appear when the wiring helps: see CNNs.

04Its mistakes

About 1 in 20 test digits is read wrongly. Some mistakes are understandable; some digits even a person would hesitate over. The confusion matrix counts, for every true digit (rows), what the network answered (columns).

05It does not know what it does not know

The network was only ever shown digits. It has no way to say “this is not a digit”: whatever you give it, ten numbers come out and one of them is the largest. Feed it random noise, or draw a letter or a smiley in § 01.

Nonsense in, an answer out

Each click makes a new picture.

Worse, networks can even memorise completely random labels: high accuracy on the training data while learning nothing that generalises. Discussion: where would this be dangerous? (A medical scan from a new machine, a self-driving car in snow, a face it has never seen.)

Key idea: always test on new data, and be careful outside the training data.
