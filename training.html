01Watch a network learn

02Scaling up: the digit network

The digit network learns in exactly the same way, just with 13,002 knobs and 60,000 examples. Take a mini-batch of 32 digits, run the forward pass, compute the cost, backpropagate, take one step. Repeat for all 60,000 digits: one epoch. We trained for 30 epochs, about a minute on a laptop.

Why about 10% before training? Random weights pick one of 10 digits more or less at random, so they are right about 1 time in 10.

07It does not know what it does not know

The network was only ever shown digits. It has no way to say “this is not a digit”: whatever you give it, ten numbers come out and one of them is the largest. Feed it random noise, or draw a letter or a smiley in the drawing pad above.

Nonsense in, an answer out

Each click makes a new picture.

Worse, networks can even memorise completely random labels: high accuracy on the training data while learning nothing that generalises. Discussion: where would this be dangerous? (A medical scan from a new machine, a self-driving car in snow, a face it has never seen.)

Key idea: always test on new data, and be careful outside the training data.
